Not every decision needs a human. Not every decision should be automated. The art is knowing where to draw the line.

I call it the 80/20 of human-in-the-loop AI.

The 80% (let agents handle it):
- Routine reorders within known parameters
- Standard exception categorization
- Data enrichment and cleanup
- Status updates and notifications
- Pattern-matching against established rules

The 20% (protect this for humans):
- Decisions with financial thresholds above tolerance
- Actions affecting customer relationships
- Anything that requires cross-functional context
- Novel situations the model hasn't seen before
- Decisions where "wrong" has consequences that compound

The mistake most teams make: they either automate everything (and lose governance) or require human approval on everything (and lose the speed advantage).

The fix: define your 80/20 line explicitly.

For every workflow, ask:
- What's the blast radius if the agent is wrong?
- How often does this decision need context the agent doesn't have?
- Can a human meaningfully evaluate this in the time available?

Agents recommend. People decide. But only on the decisions that deserve human attention.

Everything else? Let the agent run.
