The fastest way to scale a problem is to automate it.

I've watched teams take a broken process, hand it to an AI agent, and celebrate when it runs 10x faster. Congratulations. You now produce bad outcomes at machine speed.

AI amplifies whatever you give it. Good process becomes great. Bad process becomes catastrophic.

Signs you're automating a bad process:
- The humans doing it today can't explain why certain steps exist
- There are workarounds that "everyone just knows about"
- The output requires manual correction most of the time
- Nobody's questioned whether the process is still needed

Before you automate, ask:
- If we were designing this from scratch today, would we do it this way?
- What steps exist because of a constraint that no longer applies?
- What would we eliminate if we could?

Fix first. Then accelerate.

The right order:
1. Map the current process
2. Identify what's broken, redundant, or legacy
3. Redesign for the outcome you actually want
4. Then, and only then, bring in AI to accelerate

AI is not a renovation tool. It's an amplification tool. It doesn't fix what's broken. It does more of whatever already exists.

If your process is good, AI makes it exceptional. If your process is bad, AI makes it exceptionally bad. Faster.

Fix first. Automate second.
