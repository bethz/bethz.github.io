I've sat through dozens of AI demos this year. Slick. Impressive. Standing ovations from leadership.

Then nothing happens.

Demo culture is killing AI adoption. Here's the pattern:

1. Team builds a prototype in a week
2. Demo wows the room
3. Leadership says "ship it"
4. Team discovers production is 10x harder than the demo
5. Six months later, the prototype is still "almost ready"

The gap between "look what it can do" and "here's what it does every day" is enormous. And demos hide that gap perfectly.

What demos don't show:
- Error handling when data is messy (it always is)
- Performance at scale, not just with sample data
- Security, compliance, and governance requirements
- Monitoring, alerting, and fallback procedures
- What happens when the model drifts and nobody notices

Production-grade agents aren't exciting to demo. They're reliable. They're boring. They work at 2 AM when nobody's watching.

The companies actually deploying AI at scale aren't the ones with the best demos. They're the ones who asked: "What does this look like on day 100, not day 1?"

Stop celebrating demos. Start celebrating production deployments that survived their first month.
