We reward speed. Ship it. Get it done. Move fast.

But here's what I've learned working across enterprise partnerships for 20+ years: execution started too early slows teams down. It doesn't speed them up.

When teams jump to building before aligning on what to build and why, three things happen:

1. Misalignment compounds. Small gaps become architectural rewrites.
2. Constraints start driving decisions. The tool you picked on Day 1 becomes the ceiling.
3. Convergence takes longer, not less. You ship something, then spend months unwinding it.

High-performing teams I've worked with, the ones that actually deliver on time, separate three phases clearly:

- Ideation (diverge freely)
- Decision (converge deliberately)
- Execution (build with clarity)

This isn't "analysis paralysis." It's discipline. The fastest path to Done is rarely the first path to Started.

Next time someone says "let's just start building and figure it out," ask: figure out what, exactly?
