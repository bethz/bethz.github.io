We reward speed. Ship it. Get it done. Move fast.

But here's what I've learned working across enterprise partnerships for 20+ years: execution started too early slows teams down. It doesn't speed them up.

And AI amplifies this problem. Teams now spin up prototypes in hours. Copilot generates architecture docs before lunch. The gap between "idea" and "code" collapsed, but the gap between "idea" and "aligned understanding" didn't.

When teams jump to building before aligning on what to build and why, three things happen:

1. Misalignment compounds. Small gaps become architectural rewrites.
2. Constraints start driving decisions. The tool you picked on Day 1 becomes the ceiling.
3. Convergence takes longer, not less. You ship something, then spend months unwinding it.

AI makes each of these worse. You can now generate a wrong solution in minutes instead of weeks. The rework cycle accelerates, not the value cycle.

High-performing teams I've worked with separate three phases clearly:

- Ideation (diverge freely)
- Decision (converge deliberately)
- Execution (build with clarity)

AI belongs in all three. But not all at once. Use it to explore options in ideation. Use it to pressure-test in decision. Use it to accelerate in execution. The sequence matters.

The fastest path to Done is rarely the first path to Started.
