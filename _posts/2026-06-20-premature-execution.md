You're five minutes into describing a new initiative. Use cases aren't defined. Personas are fuzzy. You haven't finished the sentence.

And someone says: "We should use Kafka for that."

This is premature execution. Not jumping to building too fast (that's a separate problem). This is jumping to solutions before the problem even has shape.

It's always existed. Monday morning quarterbacks reaching for architecture diagrams before anyone's asked "who is this for?"

But AI made it worse.

Now someone feeds your half-formed idea to Copilot mid-meeting and suddenly has a "fully fleshed out" technical recommendation. Bullet points. Confidence. Based on a prompt that captured maybe 10% of the actual context.

The room pivots to debating implementation details for a problem nobody's agreed on yet.

What premature execution kills:

- Discovery. You can't explore needs if the answer's already "decided."
- Inclusion. People who haven't spoken get steamrolled by the fastest AI response.
- Quality. Solutions shaped by incomplete context are confidently wrong.

The fix isn't banning AI from meetings. It's sequencing.

Before anyone proposes how: define who, what, and why. Write the use cases. Name the personas. Then let solutions begin.

AI is an incredible accelerator. But accelerating in the wrong direction just gets you lost faster.
