AI stands for Artificial Intelligence. But lately it also stands for Any Idiot.

Any idiot can generate a strategy deck. Any idiot can ask Copilot for a solution architecture. Any idiot can produce a gorgeous 40-slide presentation in 10 minutes flat. Any idiot can create a handful of agents by lunchtime.

That's not intelligence. That's output.

Intelligence is knowing what question to ask. Intelligence is recognizing when the AI gave you a confidently wrong answer. Intelligence is the human in the loop who says "this looks complete, but it's solving the wrong problem."

I just gave a talk at ICON about human-in-the-loop agentic decisions. The core message: AI agents can do incredible work. They can reason, plan, and execute. But the moment you remove human judgment from the loop, you get speed without wisdom.

The gap isn't in what AI can produce. It's in what humans fail to evaluate.

Three things AI cannot do for you:

1. Define what matters. AI optimizes. You decide what to optimize for.
2. Read the room. AI doesn't know your stakeholder's unspoken concern.
3. Own the outcome. When it goes wrong, "the AI suggested it" isn't leadership.

The companies getting value from AI aren't generating the most output. They're pairing AI speed with human judgment at every decision point.

Don't be an Any Idiot. Be the intelligence in the loop.
