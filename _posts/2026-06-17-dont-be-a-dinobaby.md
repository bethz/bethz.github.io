I heard someone use the term "dinobaby" recently and it stuck.

A dinobaby isn't about age. It's about rigidity. It's the person in the room who says "that's how we've always done it" while the world changes around them.

I've been in tech for over two decades. I've seen entire platforms rise and fall. The people who thrive aren't the ones who mastered one tool. They're the ones who mastered adaptability.

Signs you might be turning into a dinobaby:
- You dismiss new tools without trying them
- You measure your value by what you know, not what you can learn
- You resist process changes because "the old way works fine"
- You see junior people's ideas as naive rather than fresh

The antidote? Curiosity. Intentional curiosity.

I just built an AI communication coach for myself. Not because someone told me to. Because I wanted to see what was possible. Because staying relevant means staying hungry.

The best technologists I know at 25 years in are the ones who still say "I don't know, let me figure it out."

Don't be a dinobaby. Be a perpetual beginner with decades of context.
