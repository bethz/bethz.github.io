I spent weeks teaching my AI how I work.

My writing style. My file structure. My posting rules. My team names. My preferences. Dozens of corrections, nudges, and "remember this for next time" moments.

Then I got a new laptop.

None of it came with me.

We talk about AI as a partner, a copilot, a second brain. But nobody talks about what happens when that brain gets wiped. New machine. New session. Back to square one. "I'd be happy to help!" No, you already knew how to help. Yesterday.

This is the dirty secret of AI productivity. The investment is invisible and fragile. Your skills, memories, instructions, custom agents, all of it lives in local config files that don't sync, don't back up, and don't survive a hardware swap.

So I built a system. Version-controlled skills. Dotfiles for AI. A repo that rebuilds my entire AI working environment on any machine in minutes.

Because if your AI setup isn't portable, it's not a system. It's a sandcastle.

Back up your AI brain. Or be ready to retrain it from scratch. Again.
