AI can analyze sentiment. It can summarize meetings. It can even predict outcomes based on patterns.

What it cannot do: read the room.

Reading the room means knowing:
- The CFO's concern isn't about budget, it's about control
- The SVP nodding along hasn't actually agreed to anything
- The silence from engineering isn't alignment, it's disagreement they're not surfacing
- The real decision was made in the hallway before the meeting started

No model captures this. No agent detects it. No amount of training data includes the unspoken politics of your specific organization.

This is where human-in-the-loop isn't just nice to have. It's the entire point.

AI can surface data, compare scenarios, and recommend actions. But the human in the loop provides:
- Intent (what are we actually trying to achieve here?)
- Judgment (is this the right move given what I know about these people?)
- Policy control (what are we willing to risk?)
- Relational awareness (who needs to feel heard before this moves forward?)

The best AI implementations I've seen pair agent speed with human intuition. Not as a rubber stamp, but as genuine steering.

AI gives you the what. Only you can navigate the who.
