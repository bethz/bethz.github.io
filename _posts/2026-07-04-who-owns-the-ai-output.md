Something goes wrong. The AI agent made a recommendation. The team followed it. The outcome was bad.

Who's accountable?

"The AI suggested it" isn't leadership. But I hear versions of it constantly:
- "Copilot generated that architecture"
- "The agent flagged it as low risk"
- "We followed the model's recommendation"

None of these are accountability. They're abdication dressed as innovation.

Ownership of AI output means:
- If you approve it, you own it. Period.
- If you deploy it without review, you own the outcome of skipping review.
- If you built the agent, you own its failure modes.
- If you set the guardrails, you own what happens inside them.

AI doesn't remove accountability. It redistributes it.

The governance question every team needs to answer:
- Who signs off on agent decisions above threshold X?
- Who monitors for drift and degradation?
- Who gets paged when the agent does something unexpected?
- Who explains to the customer what happened and why?

Every decision should be traceable. Every action auditable. Every outcome owned by a person, not a model.

AI agents can recommend. Only humans can be accountable.

Own your outputs.
