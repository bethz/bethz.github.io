Your AI is a strong intern. Confident. Fast. Produces clean work. Looks like they know what they're doing.

But you wouldn't let an intern sign a contract. You wouldn't let them present to the board unchecked. You wouldn't ship their code to production without a review.

So why are teams doing exactly that with AI output?

I've seen enterprise research (ELP FY26) where users consistently describe AI the same way: "like a strong intern whose work must be checked." They love the speed. They appreciate the drafts. But they explicitly say they would not rely on it unchecked.

The problem isn't the intern. It's when organizations forget the "check" part.

When does helpful become dangerous:
- When the output is so polished that checking feels unnecessary
- When the team is so busy that verification gets skipped
- When leadership rewards speed over accuracy
- When nobody asks "is this actually correct?" because it looks correct

The strong intern is valuable. But only with a strong manager.

If your AI workflow doesn't have a human verification step baked in, not optional, not "when you have time," but mandatory, then you don't have an AI assistant. You have an unsupervised intern with production access.

Manage your AI like you'd manage a brilliant but inexperienced hire. Trust, but verify. Every time.
