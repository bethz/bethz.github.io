Most teams operate in one mode: execute. Somebody says "go," and everyone starts building. When alignment breaks, they patch it mid-sprint. When scope shifts, they absorb it and ship anyway.

This works until it doesn't. And with AI accelerating execution speed, it breaks faster than ever.

The fix isn't more process. It's cleaner phases.

Phase 1: Ideate (diverge freely)
- No solutions allowed yet
- Ask "who needs this?" and "what does success look like?"
- AI's role: explore possibilities, generate options, surface what you haven't considered

Phase 2: Decide (converge deliberately)
- Choose the path. Document the why.
- Get explicit sign-off. Not "nobody objected," but "we agreed."
- AI's role: pressure-test assumptions, model scenarios, compare tradeoffs

Phase 3: Execute (build with clarity)
- Everyone knows the target. No re-litigating.
- Speed is safe because direction is set.
- AI's role: accelerate output, automate the repetitive, catch errors early

The magic isn't in any single phase. It's in keeping them separate.

When you ideate during execution, you get scope creep. When you execute during ideation, you get premature commitments. When you skip decisions entirely, you get rework.

Three phases. Clear transitions. AI in all three, but never driving the phase shift.

That's how fast teams stay aligned.
