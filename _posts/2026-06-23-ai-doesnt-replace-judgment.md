AI can write code, draft emails, summarize meetings, and generate architectures. What it cannot do is decide whether any of that should exist.

I've watched teams adopt AI tools and immediately stop thinking critically. The output looks polished. The formatting is clean. The confidence is high. So people accept it.

But polished isn't the same as correct. Confident isn't the same as right.

When to trust AI:
- Repetitive tasks with clear patterns
- First drafts you plan to edit heavily
- Summarizing information you'll verify
- Exploring options you haven't considered

When to override AI:
- Stakeholder dynamics it can't see
- Organizational context it doesn't have
- Ethical judgment calls
- Anything where "wrong but confident" has real consequences

The skill gap in AI adoption isn't prompting. It's evaluation. The people who thrive with AI are the ones who can look at a beautifully formatted output and say: "This is wrong. Here's why."

That's not a technical skill. It's a leadership skill.

AI amplifies whatever you bring to it. Bring judgment, get leverage. Bring nothing, get noise.
