The question everyone asks about AI: "What jobs will it replace?"

Wrong question.

The right question: "What can a person do when AI removes the friction?"

I've spent the last year working at the intersection of AI agents and enterprise decision-making. Here's what I've seen: the best implementations don't replace humans. They unleash them.

A supply chain planner who used to spend 4 hours pulling data now spends 4 hours making decisions. A strategist who spent days building decks now spends that time building relationships. An engineer who spent weeks on boilerplate now ships meaningful features in days.

AI doesn't give people new brains. It gives them their time back. And time is where superpowers live.

What "unleashing the human" looks like in practice:

- Human-in-the-loop agentic workflows where AI does the work and humans steer the outcome
- Copilots that surface the right context at the right moment, not just answers
- Agents that handle the 80% so people can focus on the 20% that actually requires judgment

The companies winning aren't automating humans out. They're amplifying humans up.

Give your people AI as a tool, and you get incremental efficiency. Give them AI as a superpower, and you get exponential capability.

Unleash the human.
